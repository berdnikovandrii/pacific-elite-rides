Hosting a Client Visit in San Diego: Flat-Rate Transfers or an Hourly Car? The Real Math

When a client, an investor or a visiting executive flies into San Diego, someone on your team ends up planning the ground transportation — usually the night before. The question we hear most is simple: should we book separate rides, or keep one car for the day? Here's the honest math with our published rates, including the cases where neither one is the cheapest option.

The two ways to book

  • Flat-rate transfers. One fixed price per trip, set before the ride. From San Diego International (SAN): $60 to Downtown/Gaslamp, $90 to La Jolla/UTC, $95 to Del Mar, $115 to Carlsbad and Encinitas. Flight tracking, meet & greet and 60 minutes of free wait time are included, and the price doesn't surge.
  • Hourly charter. A Chevy Suburban or Cadillac Escalade ESV with a driver for a block of time — $95/hr with a 2-hour minimum, up to 6 passengers. The car stays with you between stops.

Rides that don't start or end at the airport — say, a hotel in the Gaslamp to an office in Sorrento Valley — are quoted up front before you book, so you know every number before the visit starts.

Scenario 1: arrival and departure only

Your guest lands Tuesday, stays at a hotel near UTC, walks or takes a short ride to your office, and flies out Thursday. Book two flat-rate transfers: $90 in, $90 out — $180 total. An hourly car makes no sense here; you'd be paying for a vehicle to wait while your guest sleeps.

Scenario 2: fly in, one meeting, fly out the same day

A guest lands at 10:00 a.m., has a two-hour meeting in UTC and catches a 3:00 p.m. flight home.

  • Two flat transfers: $90 + $90 = $180.
  • Hourly: pickup at 10:00, back at SAN around 1:00 p.m. — three hours, $285.

Flat rates win by $105. The hourly car earns its premium only if the meeting might run long and nobody wants to rebook the return, or if your guest would rather leave the bags in the car than roll them into a conference room. If neither matters, book two transfers.

Scenario 3: a full day of meetings

This is where hourly service makes sense — and where it's easy to overspend. Say your visitor has three meetings on Wednesday morning in UTC, Sorrento Valley and Del Mar, lunch until 1:00 p.m., and a 5:00 p.m. flight.

  • Car all day: 9:00 a.m. pickup through the SAN drop-off around 4:00 p.m. — seven hours, $665.
  • Hourly for the busy part, flat for the airport: four hours of hourly service for the three meetings and lunch ($380), then a flat La Jolla/UTC-to-SAN transfer at 3:00 p.m. ($90) — $470.

The split saves $195, at the cost of the car not waiting during the quiet stretch after lunch. For most visits that's the better deal: pay hourly for the hours with several stops close together, and use flat rates for the airport legs.

When hosting several guests

An SUV seats up to six, so the hourly rate splits fast. A 4-hour block for a visiting team of five is $380 — $76 a person — and everyone arrives at the meeting together instead of in three separate cars. For a bigger group, an offsite or a team dinner, the Sprinter seats up to 14 at $180/hr with a 4-hour minimum ($720). For one guest you want to impress — a client dinner you're hoping to close over — the Mercedes-Maybach S-Class is $280/hr with a 3-hour minimum and seats up to three. We compared it honestly in our Maybach vs. Escalade piece; airport runs belong on the SUV flat rates either way.

When rideshare is the better call

We'd rather say it up front:

  • A solo traveler with a short hop. Hotel to an office ten minutes away on a weekday afternoon — rideshare will usually be cheaper than anything we offer.
  • Loose timing. If nobody minds waiting a few minutes for a car between meetings, separate rides cost less than keeping one.
  • A guest who prefers to handle it. Some travelers simply like their own app. That's fine.

Where rideshare gets risky is the trip that can't go wrong: the airport pickup for a guest you've been courting for months, the 7:00 a.m. ride to an investor meeting, the return to SAN when the last meeting ran 20 minutes over. A booked car with a fixed price takes that variable out of the day.

Planning tips that actually save money

  • Send us the whole itinerary at once. Flights, addresses and meeting times in one message let us suggest where hourly makes sense and where flat transfers are cheaper.
  • Book hourly in blocks that match the busy hours, not the whole visit.
  • Add the flight number to every airport leg. Flight tracking moves the pickup if the flight is late, with no rebooking.
  • If your team does this often, set up a corporate account with direct billing — the details are in our corporate car service guide.
