Carlsbad, Encinitas or Oceanside to San Diego Airport: COASTER, Parking, Rideshare or a Flat-Rate Car? The Real Cost

If you live in Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach, Del Mar or Oceanside, getting to San Diego International (SAN) is a 30-to-40-mile trip down the I-5 — far enough that the choice matters, close enough that people often decide the night before. There are four realistic ways to do it: the COASTER train plus the free airport shuttle, driving and parking, rideshare, or a booked car at a flat rate. Here's what each one actually costs from North County, using published numbers only, and when each one wins — including when it isn't us.

Option 1: COASTER + the free San Diego Flyer

The COASTER commuter train stops in Oceanside, Carlsbad, Encinitas and Solana Beach and runs south to Old Town. From the Old Town Transit Center, the airport's free San Diego Flyer shuttle runs to Terminal 1 and Terminal 2. The fare is a few dollars per adult each way; NCTD changed its fares on October 1, 2026, so check the current figure on gonctd.com before you go.

  • Wins when: you're traveling alone or as a couple, light on bags, on a midday flight, and you live within walking distance or a short drop-off of a station.
  • Loses when: the flight leaves early in the morning (check that the first southbound train reaches Old Town with enough time to spare), you're carrying two large cases, a stroller and a car seat, or you have small children who've been up since 4 a.m. Two vehicles, one transfer and a walk with every bag add up fast.

For a solo traveler with a backpack, it's the cheapest way to SAN by a wide margin. We'd take it too.

Option 2: drive and park at SAN

San Diego International's Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 parking plazas charge $3 per 15 minutes with a $40 daily maximum, and curbside valet is $60 per day (per san.org; reservations open up to 30 days ahead). Private off-airport lots usually cost less but add a shuttle ride on each end.

  • A 3-day trip in the plaza: up to $120 in parking, plus fuel for about 70–80 miles of round-trip driving.
  • A 7-day trip: up to $280 in the plaza, or $420 with valet.

Driving yourself is hard to beat for a short trip — and you control the timing. It gets expensive on a week away, and it means driving home on the I-5 after a long flight.

Option 3: rideshare

Rideshare prices change by the minute, so we won't quote a number we can't stand behind — open the app at the hour you'd actually leave and compare. For one traveler with a carry-on at a quiet time of day, UberX from Encinitas or Carlsbad will often come in below our flat rate. That's an honest win for rideshare.

It gets less predictable when you need an XL vehicle for luggage or more than four people, when you need car seats (most rideshare cars don't carry them), or when your pickup time is 4:30 a.m. and you're relying on a driver being nearby at that hour. Our deeper comparison of what actually fits in each vehicle is in Suburban luggage capacity vs. Uber.

Option 4: a flat-rate car

Our published SAN rates from North County, per vehicle, each way:

  • Del Mar: $95
  • Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach: $115
  • Escondido, Rancho Santa Fe: $120
  • Oceanside, Vista, San Marcos: $130

Every rate covers up to 6 passengers with luggage in a Cadillac Escalade ESV or Chevy Suburban. Fuel, tolls, flight tracking, meet & greet and 60 minutes of free wait time are included, the first child seat is free, and the price is the same at 5 a.m., on a holiday weekend or in a rainstorm. What's included is broken down in full in our flat-rate explainer.

Three real scenarios, priced

A solo business trip from Carlsbad, three days

Drive and park: up to $120 in the plaza plus fuel. Car both ways: $115 + $115 = $230. Parking wins on cost. The case for the car is only that you can work on the way and don't drive home tired — whether that's worth about $100 is your call.

A family of four from Encinitas, one week

Drive and park: up to $280 in the plaza for seven days, plus fuel, and someone still has to unload four people and their bags at the curb. Car both ways: $230 total — about $29 a person per direction — with the car seats fitted before pickup. The COASTER is cheaper on fares but means two vehicles, a transfer and every suitcase carried by hand. Here the flat rate is usually the better deal on both money and effort.

Six colleagues from Oceanside to an early flight

One car at $130 works out to about $22 a person, everyone arrives together, and nobody leaves a car at the airport for the week. Separate rideshares or separate cars cost more and split the team across several cars at the curb.

When to leave North County for SAN

For a domestic flight we normally collect about two and a half hours before departure from Encinitas (35–45 minutes of driving on a clear road) and Carlsbad (40–50 minutes), and about three hours before from Oceanside (45–55 minutes). Add an hour for an international flight. Weekday mornings southbound on the I-5 are the variable — the I-5 is the only practical route, and one accident changes the whole drive. Send us the flight number and we work backwards from it.

Coming home works the other way round: we track the inbound flight, so if it lands late the pickup moves with it at no charge, and your free wait starts at touchdown.

The short version

  • Alone, light bags, flexible time: COASTER + San Diego Flyer, or rideshare.
  • Short trip and you don't mind driving: park at SAN.
  • A week or longer, a family with bags and car seats, a group, or an early flight with no slack: a flat-rate car usually costs less than parking and removes the guesswork.
