SAN Airport to the San Diego Cruise Terminal With All Your Luggage: Timing, Drop-Off and What It Costs

San Diego Airport (SAN) sits about two miles from the cruise piers, so cruise day looks simple on a map — until you add up checked bags for a week at sea, a carousel that takes its time, a sailing day when several thousand people are heading for the same stretch of North Harbor Drive, and a ship that will not wait. Here's how long the ride really takes, when to leave the airport, where you're dropped, and what it costs — including when a rideshare or taxi is cheaper.

First, what we do and don't do with luggage

A lot of people search for "baggage delivery to the San Diego cruise terminal" or "left luggage at the cruise port." To be straight about it: we move people together with their luggage. You and your bags ride in the same Escalade ESV or Suburban, from the carousel at SAN to the pier. We don't carry suitcases on their own without their owners, and we don't store luggage.

If what you need is bags shipped ahead or held for a few hours without you, that's a luggage-shipping or luggage-storage service, which is a different business. If you're staying downtown the night before or after your cruise, ask your hotel's front desk whether they'll hold bags for guests — many do — and ask your cruise line what it offers for your sailing.

Which pier, and where you're dropped

Your cruise documents name the terminal. Most ships use the B Street Cruise Ship Terminal at 1140 N Harbor Drive; some dock at the Port Pavilion on Broadway Pier, 1000 N Harbor Drive, a few minutes south. Both are the same flat rate from SAN.

We drop you past the gate, on the pier, as the Port recommends — not at the back of the line of cars on Harbor Drive. Your chauffeur unloads every bag, and the cruise line's porters take the ones you've tagged. Those are delivered to your cabin later in the day, so keep passports, medication and whatever you need for the first afternoon in your carry-on.

How long the ride takes, and when to leave SAN

On an ordinary day, SAN to the pier is about 12 minutes along Harbor Drive. On a sailing morning, give it more: the last stretch near the terminal backs up when several ships turn around on the same day. The drive is rarely the problem; everything before it is:

  • Deplaning and baggage claim: with checked bags for two to six people, plan on 30–45 minutes after landing, sometimes longer.
  • The ride: about 12 minutes, 20–30 on a busy sailing morning.
  • Drop-off and handing bags to the porters: a few minutes once you're on the pier.

Work backwards from the boarding time on your cruise documents, not from the sail time. If your flight lands on the morning of sailing, that buffer is thin, which is why most cruisers fly in the day before; if you do, we can take you from your hotel to the pier instead. If you're flying in the same day, send us the flight number — we track it, so a late landing moves the pickup with it, and your 60 minutes of free wait starts at touchdown, not at the scheduled time.

What it costs with us

  • SAN → B Street Cruise Ship Terminal or Broadway Pier: $60 flat, per vehicle, each way, up to 6 passengers with their luggage.
  • LAX → the San Diego pier: $375 flat, same vehicle, same terms.
  • Included: meet & greet at baggage claim, every bag loaded and unloaded for you, flight tracking and 60 minutes of free wait.

The price doesn't move on a Saturday sailing or a holiday. Details for this route are on our SAN airport to cruise port page.

Rideshare, taxi or a flat-rate car: honest math for 2 and for 6

Two people, a suitcase each

Our car is $60, or $30 a person. For a trip this short, a standard rideshare or a metered taxi from the official SAN taxi line will often come in under $60 for a couple with two cases — check the app at the time you'd actually leave. If you're traveling light with time to spare, take it.

The car earns its $60 when the margin is thin: someone already waiting at baggage claim, bags loaded for you, and a drop past the gate rather than at the end of the curb line.

Six people, a week of luggage

Six people on a cruise usually means six large suitcases plus carry-ons. An XL rideshare is rated for six passengers, but six people and that much luggage rarely fit in one, so most groups end up in two cars — two fares and half the group waiting at the pier for the other half. Our full comparison of what actually fits is in Suburban luggage capacity vs. Uber.

One Escalade ESV at $60 is $10 a person, everyone arrives together, and nobody stands at the curb guarding bags. If your group has more than a large suitcase and a carry-on each, or golf bags, tell us when you book and we'll send a second SUV.

Flying into LAX instead

Cruise fares and flights sometimes work out cheaper through Los Angeles. From LAX it's $375 flat to the San Diego pier — about $62 a person for six — met at baggage claim. The drive is roughly two to three hours depending on I-5 traffic, so book it for the day before sailing if you possibly can; a missed ship cannot be rebooked. More on that route on our LAX to San Diego car service page.

Coming home: disembarkation day

Disembarkation runs to the ship's schedule, not yours. Book a pickup time based on your disembarkation group, then text us when you're off the ship. The 60 minutes of free wait covers a slow gangway or a customs hold, and we go straight to SAN, a downtown hotel or home — the same $60 back to the airport.

If your flight isn't until the evening, the cheapest answer is usually a hotel bag hold or a luggage-storage service. An hourly SUV ($95/hr, 2-hour minimum) keeps the car — and your bags — with you while you see the city, but four hours is $380, so it only makes sense for a group that wants the car anyway.

The short version

  • Two people, light bags, plenty of time: a rideshare or taxi is cheaper. Take it.
  • A family or group with a week of luggage, or a same-day flight with little slack: $60 flat for up to six people, met at baggage claim, dropped past the gate.
  • Bags without passengers, or storage: not us — use a luggage-shipping or storage service, or your hotel.
